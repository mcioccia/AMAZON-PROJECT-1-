## Proposed research lens: capacity must earn its cost

The distinctive research question is not simply whether AWS can grow. It is whether the operating profit and cash flow created by that growth justify the capacity investment needed to deliver it. Higher revenue and greater investment must be examined together. Amazon combines retail operations, cloud infrastructure and private investments, so a single consolidated growth rate or headline earnings multiple can conceal materially different cash demands.

The proposed stance is watch/defer until capital productivity can be defended. ## Three research distinctions worth defending

1. **Demand versus capacity:** additional cloud revenue may require additional servers, power, buildings and leases. Test revenue growth alongside capital spending and depreciation rather than assuming growth is free.
2. **Accounting earnings versus cash reinvestment:** a recognized investment gain does not establish recurring operating cash generation. Forecast operating performance separately from private-investment value in the equity bridge.
3. **Terminal profitability versus terminal upkeep:** a long-run margin is only useful if the assumed reinvestment can sustain it. A terminal growth/ROIC formula needs a credible transition from the explicit investment cycle.

These are proposed analytical distinctions, not the student's required AI-off highest-risk claim or three failures. That separate exercise remains for the student to complete without assistance.

## Working assumptions and reasons to challenge them

| Assumption | Proposed rationale | Evidence needed to retain or revise it |
|---|---|---|
| AWS initial growth 24%, fading two percentage points a year | Recent growth anchors the first year; maturation tempers later growth | Capacity utilization, backlog conversion, pricing and customer demand |
| 2026 cash-investment anchor $200bn | Management's capital expenditure outlook is a starting reference | Reconcile definition to cash additions, leases and timing; do not count the same assets twice |
| Later capex responds to AWS growth with elasticity 0.65 | Some shared infrastructure may let demand outgrow investment | Measured investment intensity and asset utilization; 0.65 is a judgment, not an estimated coefficient |
| Six-year blended asset life | A tractable first model of mixed assets | Asset-category lives, vintage schedules and retirement patterns |
| 10% discount rate | A transparent initial hurdle for sensitivity analysis | Date-matched risk-free rate, equity risk premium, beta, debt cost and financing weights |
| Terminal growth 3%, incremental ROIC 15% | Connect ongoing growth to required reinvestment | Sustainable returns and maintenance/growth spending consistent with the terminal margin |

Segment pre-depreciation margins, taxes, liquidity, dilution and private-investment treatment are also load-bearing assumptions. See the complete challenge record; none becomes a fact merely because it is an input.
